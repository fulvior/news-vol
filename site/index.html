Can the news explain volatility?

Every morning this page collects the previous day’s news coverage from GDELT and sets it against market volatility in the United States and Europe. Pick a market and a news theme to see how closely the two move together.

What moves volatility, day by day

A statistical model first predicts each day’s volatility from its own recent history and from recent falls in the stock market. The bars below show how much the day’s news then pushed volatility above or below that prediction, topic by topic. Select a day to read the headlines behind it.

Each bar is the news model’s estimate of how much each topic raised (above zero) or lowered (below zero) volatility that day, compared with a day of usual news. The line is the total. Over longer periods, bars are weekly or monthly averages.

Which news matters

The effect on volatility of one standard deviation more coverage of a topic (attention), or of a more stressed tone in that coverage (tone), with 95% bootstrap intervals. Filled dots stay significant after adjusting for testing many topics at once.

How to read this

Share of coverage
Out of all articles published that day by outlets in the market’s region, the percentage that GDELT tags with the chosen theme. It measures how much attention a topic gets.
Tone
GDELT’s average tone score for those articles. Below zero means more negative than positive language.
Implied and realized volatility
The VIX and the VSTOXX are implied by option prices and look about a month ahead. Realized volatility is estimated from each day’s open, high, low and closing prices and expressed at an annual rate.
The correlation
It compares day-to-day changes in the two series over the period shown. It describes how they move together and says nothing yet about which one leads.

What comes next

Further model families: GARCH models of daily returns with news in the variance equation, and a model that separates short-lived volatility from a slow-moving component driven by sustained coverage.

Volatility data: Cboe Global Markets, STOXX Ltd., Yahoo Finance. News data: the GDELT Project, a sample of its Global Knowledge Graph. Regions are defined by where each outlet publishes: the United States, or the United Kingdom, Germany, France, Italy and Spain for Europe.
